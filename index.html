Ship It: Data Platform Migration

Move a warehouse without breaking the numbers. Serai Commerce, a constructed Southeast Asian commerce enabler, is moving its warehouse from a legacy stack to a new one; underneath are the same 8,614 real orders from the public Olist dataset its sibling courses use. Six leader sessions decide what to move, in what order, which differences to accept, when to cut over and when to switch the old system off. Ten builder sessions do the move: inventory from the query log, the dependency graph, SQL translation, row, key and aggregate parity, historical reload, a parallel run, a rehearsed rollback, the drift that hides the week after, and a parity bench computed in your browser. On cutover day, can you prove every number the business reads is the same, or say exactly why it changed?

Leader track 🧭 · six sessions, no code

For heads of data and platform owners asked to approve or run a warehouse move. Decide the scope, the order, the bar the numbers must clear, the cutover call and the day the old system goes dark.

Why migrations break the numbers

Inventory before plan

What you own, who reads it and what nobody reads. In Serai's constructed estate, 83 of 214 tables went unread for 90 days.

One decision per asset, and the order of moves. Big bang against a phased, parallel move, taught as the disagreement it is.

The parity bar

Go/no-go, the freeze, and rollback criteria written while everyone is calm, with two real incidents read closely.

The week after and switching off

The drift that hides inside a month, the decommission log, and why the cost case only closes when the old system is off.

Builder track 🔧 · ten sessions, SQL in DuckDB and the browser

The move itself, in the order a careful team does it: find out what is used, order the moves, translate, prove parity three ways, reload, run in parallel, rehearse, cut over and watch.

🔎 Builder session 1 · start here

Inventory from the query log

Usage and lineage from 90 days of a constructed query log in DuckDB: which tables are read, by whom, and which are dead.

▶ Start here 🕸️ Builder session 2

The dependency graph and the order of moves

Turn the inventory into a graph and the graph into an order: leaves first, shared sources last, cycles named.

 🔤 Builder session 3

Translate the SQL

Dialect gaps that compile and still change the answer: time zones, NULL, integer division and collation, each run in DuckDB.

 🔑 Builder session 4

Row and key parity

Counts catch 1 of 5 planted defects. Keys add the 1,767 orders whose customer key no longer resolves.

 📊 Builder session 5

Six metrics by day and by month, with tolerances by metric, and why exact equality on floating point is an alarm machine.

 📦 Builder session 6

Historical reload

Reloading history as one cutover step, and the 367 late rows that came back twice.

 🛤️ Builder session 7

Parallel run

Two systems, one truth: the diff dashboard that runs every night until the numbers stop moving.

Cutover rehearsal and rollback

Rehearse the cutover end to end, time it, and test the rollback as hard as the move.

The week after: drift that hides

 🎛️ Builder session 10

The parity bench

Seven planted drifts on 8,614 real orders, five rungs of checks, a loose bar and a break button. Capstone: Serai's signed runbook.
