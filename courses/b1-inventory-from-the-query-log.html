Ship It: Data Platform Migration · Builder session 1 of 10

Inventory from the query log

A table list tells you what exists. A migration needs to know what is used: which tables are read, by whom, what feeds them, and which nobody has opened in months. Every warehouse keeps that answer in its query or access history. Tonight you turn a constructed slice of Serai Commerce's log into the first section of the migration runbook, in DuckDB, with five queries: reads per table, dead tables, lineage, people or jobs, and a status for every table.

Part 1 · covers usage from the query log

Reads, not tables

5 min live

The cheapest table to migrate is the one you do not move. To know which, count what is read, not what exists, and separate reads by people and dashboards from reads by the jobs that build other tables.

LiveWhat a query log holds, and the shape to ask for3 min▶

Most warehouses keep a history of the queries they ran and, in some form, which objects each one touched. The column names differ by engine and so does the retention, so the first job is to get it into one shape: one row per table per query, with the query id, when it ran, who or what ran it, and whether it read or wrote that table. Tonight's log is already in that shape.

ColumnWhy the inventory needs it
query_idJoins the reads and writes of one query, which is how lineage falls out
run_atThe window: last 90 days, and the last read per table
actorSeparates jobs (job:) from people and dashboards
actionread or write
table_nameThe object, schema included

If your engine only keeps query text, the table names have to be parsed out of the SQL. That works, with care for views and dynamic SQL, and it is slower. Ask the platform owner which history exists and how far back it goes before you promise an inventory date.

Self-studySerai's full estate, in constructed numbers2 min▶

Tonight's slice has 14 tables so every row fits on a screen. The constructed full estate the leader track plans against has 214 tables, 61 scheduled jobs and 38 dashboards; its 90-day log shows 131 tables read at least once, 83 never read, and 19 dashboards with no viewer. Those are teaching numbers for the case, not a benchmark: estates vary enormously, and nobody has published a reliable share of dead tables in a typical warehouse.

Real world

Vendors put discovery first. A 2019 Google Cloud blog on moving data warehouses names its phases "Prepare and discover", "Assess and plan" and "Execute", and argues for moving iteratively, use case by use case. Read it as a vendor describing its own process, not a recommendation. The shape is still right: you cannot plan a move of things you have not counted, and the query log is the cheapest honest count.

Part 2 · covers lineage from read-then-write queries

Lineage falls out of the log

5 min live

A query that reads table A and writes table B is an edge from A to B. Join the log to itself on the query id and you have a lineage graph without a lineage tool, good enough to plan a move.

LiveWhy "read only by jobs" still moves3 min▶

No person has read raw.reviews in 90 days, and it still has to move: it feeds stg.reviews, which feeds brand_review, which the brand review dashboard reads. A table earns its move through the tables downstream of it. That is why the inventory needs lineage, and why "never read by a person" alone is the wrong retirement test.

The converse is the useful find. mart.seller_scorecard_v1 is written every night by the mart job and read by nothing at all. Retiring it saves its storage, its compute every night, and one more table to translate, reload and prove in the new warehouse.

Self-studyWhere a log-derived lineage goes wrong3 min▶

Reads that happen outside the warehouse are invisible: a spreadsheet that pulled an extract last year, a partner who receives a file. Views can hide the base tables a query really read, depending on how your engine logs them. And a 90-day window misses anything read quarterly or yearly, such as an annual report or an audit pack. Treat the log as the first draft, then confirm every retire candidate with an owner. Full lineage tooling belongs to the metadata world; the Metadata, Catalog and Lineage course covers it properly.

Real world

Validation tools assume you know your keys and your tables. AWS's Database Migration Service documentation says its data validation "requires that the table has a primary key or unique index". Google's open-source Data Validation Tool runs column, row, schema and custom-query validation against tables you name. Neither can tell you which tables matter. The inventory is the input every validation step after it depends on.

Part 3 · covers retire candidates and how to retire safely

Dead is a question, not a verdict

5 min live

Three tables on Serai's slice had no read in the window. Each one needs a different next step, and none of them is "drop it tonight".

LiveA retirement that can be undone3 min▶

Retire in two steps. First, stop writing and revoke reads, leaving the data in place, and announce the date to everyone who ever read it. Second, after an agreed quiet period with no complaint, archive a copy outside the warehouse and drop the table. The first step is reversible in minutes; the second is cheap to reverse from the archive. Builder session 2 puts the retirements first in the order of moves, because every table retired is one less to translate and prove.

Self-studyDead dashboards, not just dead tables2 min▶

The same query runs over dashboards if your BI tool logs views. A dashboard with no viewer in 90 days keeps its whole lineage alive: every table it reads looks "used" because the dashboard refreshes it. Retire the dead dashboard and re-run the inventory; tables that were only read by its refresh job drop into the retire column. In Serai's constructed full estate, 19 of 38 dashboards had no viewer.

Real world

The vendor guides agree on one thing about scope. Google Cloud's 2019 migration blog asks teams to validate each use case "according to your definition of done" before moving on. A table that serves no use case has no definition of done, which is the strongest argument for retiring it before the move rather than proving it afterwards.

Build-along

Five queries, one inventory

★ 22 min · everyone builds

You need DuckDB: pip install duckdb and a Python prompt, or the DuckDB command-line shell. Paste the setup once, then run the five queries in order. Every output below is what DuckDB prints on this exact log.

Load the constructed log. Fourteen tables and 26 log rows from Serai's warehouse, with the window ending 2026-11-15.

★ Setup (paste once)CREATE OR REPLACE TABLE tables AS SELECT * FROM (VALUES ('raw.orders_export_a'),('raw.orders_export_b'),('raw.orders_export_c'),('raw.reviews'), ('ref.state_region'),('stg.orders'),('stg.reviews'),('mart.orders_daily'),('mart.orders_monthly'), ('mart.brand_review'),('mart.seller_scorecard_v1'),('mart.seller_scorecard_v2'), ('mart.region_sales_2019'),('tmp.orders_backup_2017')) t(table_name); CREATE OR REPLACE TABLE query_log AS SELECT query_id, CAST(run_at AS DATE) AS run_at, actor, action, table_name FROM (VALUES (1,'2026-11-14','job:build_stg','read','raw.orders_export_a'), (1,'2026-11-14','job:build_stg','read','raw.orders_export_b'), (1,'2026-11-14','job:build_stg','read','raw.orders_export_c'), (1,'2026-11-14','job:build_stg','write','stg.orders'), (2,'2026-11-14','job:build_stg','read','raw.reviews'), (2,'2026-11-14','job:build_stg','write','stg.reviews'), (3,'2026-11-14','job:build_marts','read','stg.orders'), (3,'2026-11-14','job:build_marts','write','mart.orders_daily'), (4,'2026-11-14','job:build_marts','read','mart.orders_daily'), (4,'2026-11-14','job:build_marts','write','mart.orders_monthly'), (5,'2026-11-14','job:build_marts','read','stg.orders'), (5,'2026-11-14','job:build_marts','read','stg.reviews'), (5,'2026-11-14','job:build_marts','read','ref.state_region'), (5,'2026-11-14','job:build_marts','write','mart.brand_review'), (6,'2026-11-14','job:build_marts','read','stg.orders'), (6,'2026-11-14','job:build_marts','write','mart.seller_scorecard_v2'), (7,'2026-11-14','job:build_marts','read','stg.orders'), (7,'2026-11-14','job:build_marts','write','mart.seller_scorecard_v1'), (8,'2026-11-01','dash:board_pack','read','mart.orders_monthly'), (9,'2026-11-14','dash:ops_daily','read','mart.orders_daily'), (10,'2026-11-13','dash:ops_daily','read','mart.orders_daily'), (11,'2026-11-03','dash:brand_review','read','mart.brand_review'), (12,'2026-10-20','user:analyst_1','read','mart.seller_scorecard_v2'), (13,'2026-09-02','user:analyst_2','read','stg.orders'), (14,'2026-05-10','user:analyst_1','read','tmp.orders_backup_2017'), (15,'2026-03-01','job:old_export','read','mart.region_sales_2019') ) q(query_id, run_at, actor, action, table_name);

Q1, reads per table in the window. stg.orders leads with 5 reads by 2 readers. mart.seller_scorecard_v1 appears with 0 reads, because the log holds its writes.

★ Q1 · reads, readers, last readSELECT table_name, count(*) FILTER (WHERE action = 'read') AS reads, count(DISTINCT actor) FILTER (WHERE action = 'read') AS readers, max(run_at) FILTER (WHERE action = 'read') AS last_read FROM query_log WHERE run_at > DATE '2026-11-15' - INTERVAL 90 DAY GROUP BY table_name ORDER BY reads DESC, table_name; -- stg.orders 5 | 2 | 2026-11-14 ... mart.seller_scorecard_v1 0 | 0 | NULL (12 rows)

Q2, tables with no read in the window. Start from the table list, not the log, or a table nobody ever touched cannot appear at all.

★ Q2 · retire candidatesSELECT t.table_name FROM tables t LEFT JOIN query_log q ON q.table_name = t.table_name AND q.action = 'read' AND q.run_at > DATE '2026-11-15' - INTERVAL 90 DAY WHERE q.query_id IS NULL ORDER BY t.table_name; -- mart.region_sales_2019, mart.seller_scorecard_v1, tmp.orders_backup_2017

Q3, lineage edges. Join reads to writes on the same query id. Eleven edges, the graph in Part 2.

★ Q3 · source to targetSELECT DISTINCT r.table_name AS source, w.table_name AS target FROM query_log r JOIN query_log w ON w.query_id = r.query_id AND w.action = 'write' WHERE r.action = 'read' ORDER BY target, source; -- 11 rows, from ref.state_region -> mart.brand_review to raw.reviews -> stg.reviews

Q4, people or jobs. Any reader whose actor does not start with job: counts as a person or a dashboard. Five tables are read by people, six only by jobs.

★ Q4 · read by people?SELECT table_name, bool_or(actor NOT LIKE 'job:%') AS read_by_people FROM query_log WHERE action = 'read' AND run_at > DATE '2026-11-15' - INTERVAL 90 DAY GROUP BY table_name ORDER BY read_by_people DESC, table_name; -- true: brand_review, orders_daily, orders_monthly, scorecard_v2, stg.orders

Q5, one status per table. This is runbook section 1. "Move as a dependency" is right here because all six job-only tables sit upstream of a table people read; in a bigger estate, check that against Q3 before you trust it.

★ Q5 · the inventoryWITH r AS ( SELECT table_name, bool_or(actor NOT LIKE 'job:%') AS people FROM query_log WHERE action = 'read' AND run_at > DATE '2026-11-15' - INTERVAL 90 DAY GROUP BY table_name ) SELECT CASE WHEN r.people THEN 'move' WHEN NOT r.people THEN 'move as dependency' ELSE 'retire candidate' END AS status, count(*) AS tables, string_agg(t.table_name, ', ' ORDER BY t.table_name) AS which FROM tables t LEFT JOIN r USING (table_name) GROUP BY status ORDER BY status; -- move 5 | move as dependency 6 | retire candidate 3

Note the NULL in Q5. A table with no read has no row in r, so r.people is NULL, and neither r.people nor NOT r.people is true: it falls through to the ELSE branch. That is the behaviour you want here, and the same three-valued logic is what Builder session 3 shows breaking a migration.

Check yourself

Three questions

Clear all three and Builder session 1 gets stamped in your passport.

1 · raw.reviews has no reader except a job. What does the inventory say?

A table earns its move through what is downstream of it. The lineage from Q3 shows the chain to a table people read, so it moves, but only because of that chain.

2 · Why does Q2 start from the table list and LEFT JOIN the log, instead of querying the log alone?

The deadest tables are exactly the ones with no log rows. Starting from the log would make them invisible, and they are the cheapest win in the whole migration.

3 · tmp.orders_backup_2017 was last read on 2026-05-10 by an analyst. What is the safe next step?

A person used it this year, so the window alone is not enough. A two-step retirement is reversible at each step and costs almost nothing.
