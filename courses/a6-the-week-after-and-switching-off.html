Ship It: Data Platform Migration · Leader session 6 of 6

The week after and switching off

Readers are on the new warehouse, the cutover went to plan, and the legacy stack sits read-only in case you need it back. This is the stretch where migrations quietly go wrong: a drift that every monthly number hides, late rows still arriving for orders placed before the switch, a cached report still serving the old figures. Tonight you write Serai Commerce's watch list, decide in writing when the watch ends, and keep the decommission log that takes the old warehouse from read-only to off. The saving the move promised starts on that last line, not on cutover day.

Part 1 · covers the drift watch after cutover

The watch, and when it ends

7 min live

Cutover moved the readers. It did not prove the readers will see the right numbers next week. Some drifts only show at a grain nobody compared closely, some only arrive after the switch, and some sit in places the parity suite never reads. The watch is a short list of exactly those, checked on purpose, with a written rule for when you stop.

LiveWhat Serai watches, and at which grain3 min▶
Watch itemGrainWhy it can still be wrong after cutoverWhat the bench measured
Orders near local midnightDailyA fixed offset is right for most of the year and wrong in summer time, and the monthly bar cannot see it72 of 595 near-midnight orders on another day; 74 days differ, 0 months
The late-row feedNightly, by keyOrders placed before cutover keep coming back on the late feed afterwards, now into the new warehouse367 orders delivered more than 30 days after purchase; appended twice, they make 8,981 rows from 8,614
Caches and extractsEach oneA report cache or a scheduled extract can keep serving legacy numbers to a reader who has "moved"Not measured: list them by name and confirm what each reads
The first month-end closeMonthlyThe first time finance reads the new warehouse under deadlineSerai's signed bar from session 4, unchanged

The rule behind the list: watch where the parity suite was weakest, not where it was strongest. Serai's monthly bar caught everything except the time-zone drift, so the watch reads that one daily. Your list will be different, and it should be just as short.

LiveA watch ends on a written condition, not a feeling3 min▶

A watch with no end either fades away unannounced or runs forever and gets ignored. Write the end into the runbook before cutover: what has to be true, for how long, and who says so. Serai's condition, a constructed policy: the rung-4 suite clean on 30 consecutive nights, the late feed's duplicated-key count at zero every night of that run, the first month-end close signed inside the bar, and every cache and extract on the list confirmed as reading the new warehouse.

How long is long enough is the same open question as the length of a parallel run. No source gives a number; Serai's 30 nights is constructed, and a business with a quarterly close might reasonably wait for that close. What is not open is that the number is written down, with an owner, before anyone is tired of watching.

Self-studyThe watch is a comparison, not a monitor3 min▶

Everything on Serai's list compares two things: the new warehouse against legacy while legacy is still loaded and read-only, or against a rule that encodes what legacy would have said, like the edge check on orders near midnight. That is the side this session owns. Designing lasting monitors on single tables in the new warehouse, freshness and volume and schema, with alert routing and on-call, belongs to the Learn Data Observability course. When the watch ends, the checks worth keeping become monitors there; the comparison against legacy stops, because legacy is about to be switched off.

Real world

A time-zone rule that changed under everyone's feet. The IANA time zone database's 2019b release records that "Brazil no longer observes DST". Code written since then can hard-code a -03:00 offset and pass every test on recent data, because recent data has no summer time. Only history from before 2019 shows the difference, and a migration reloads exactly that history. Serai's 72 orders all come from the summers of 2016-17 and 2017-18.

Part 2 · covers the decommission log

Switching off, one dated step at a time

6 min live

Switching off a warehouse is not one action. It is five, in an order where each step is harder to undo than the one before. The decommission log records each step with a date, an owner, the evidence that made it safe, and how to undo it, so that nobody has to remember in a year why the old system disappeared.

LiveThe five steps, and what each one needs first3 min▶

1, read-only. At cutover, people lose write access to legacy, but its loads keep running, so it stays current and the rollback from session 5 is real. 2, the rollback window ends. Not a date picked in advance: it closes on session 5's written condition, the first month-end close on new passing a rung-4 run with every monthly signer saying "same" or "explained", and the sponsor who owns the done sheet signs it. The week-after watch runs alongside and can close later. Before this step, a bad week means switching readers back; after it, it means fixing forward. 3, final snapshot and archive. One last copy outside both warehouses, with the totals of the six metrics recorded beside it, so a question in a year can be answered from the archive. 4, revoke every grant. This is session 2's stop-and-revoke at full scale: if someone shouts, you found a reader the inventory missed, and granting access back takes minutes. 5, switch off. Jobs, compute, service accounts, network connections, after a quiet period with no complaint.

LiveWhat a log line must say2 min▶

Each line answers five questions: when, who did it, what evidence made it safe, how you would undo it, and who signed. "Evidence" is specific: for step 2, the watch list with every item closed; for step 3, the snapshot's totals against the last parity report; for step 4, the list of who asked for access back, and what was done for each. A line without evidence is an open item, exactly as it is on the done sheet.

Self-studyWho still reads legacy the week after3 min▶

The inventory from session 2 came from a 90-day query log, and session 2 called that window a judgement. The readers it misses are the rare ones: a quarterly regulatory extract, a spreadsheet with a saved connection, a partner feed that runs once a year, a service account nobody remembers creating. Step 4 is designed to surface them while the cost is still a phone call. Serai's quiet period after revoking is 60 days, the same constructed figure it used for retirements in session 2: long enough to span one month-end close and its reruns. Set yours from the slowest reporting cycle you know of, and if that is annual, say so in the log and decide whether to wait or to archive and accept the risk in writing.

Real world

Why a list beats memory. Google's SRE book, describing how it runs launches, says that "The checklist proved to be a useful tool for ensuring reproducibly reliable launches", and that "Almost all updates to Google's services proceed gradually, according to a defined process". A decommission is a launch in reverse: a change to production with readers, data and access at stake. Writing it as dated, owned steps is the same discipline applied to taking something away.

Part 3 · covers the cost case and the last parity report

The saving starts at switch-off

6 min live

The business case for the move was a lower or better cost of running data. During the move you pay for more, not less: the new warehouse while it is built, both warehouses through the parallel run, and the legacy one again for every week it stays read-only. The case is closed on the day the log's last line is signed, and not one day before.

LiveEvery week read-only is a week paid twice3 min▶

Read the figure as a leader. The parallel run is the expensive stretch, and everyone expects it. The stretch people forget is after cutover: the new warehouse carries the readers, the legacy one is idle, and it still costs licences, storage, support contracts and the people who keep it ready for a rollback. A watch with no written end turns into a legacy warehouse nobody dares switch off, and the saving that justified the move never arrives.

The figure carries no numbers on purpose. Real warehouse prices vary by contract and change often, and no verified figure supports a general claim about how much a move saves. The shape holds regardless: the saving cannot start while you are paying for both. Whether the new line ends below the old one at all is the business case's claim, and the first full quarter after switch-off is when you can check it.

LiveThe last parity report before the archive3 min▶

Once legacy is off, you can never run the comparison again. The last parity report before step 3 is the evidence that the move was right, and it is only evidence if it says what each side read. On the bench, point both sides of the comparison at the new warehouse, the mistake a copied connection string makes, and every check passes: 0 of 5 defects caught at every rung, with every light green. Before the sponsor signs step 2, the report in front of them must name both targets, the rung and the bar, the same three things the go/no-go list asked for on cutover day.

Self-studyVendors describe the parallel stretch as a feature2 min▶

A 2019 Google Cloud blog on migrating data warehouses describes an iterative approach in which "both your data warehouse and BigQuery can be actively used in parallel". Read it as a vendor's claim about its own product, and as an honest description of the cost: actively used in parallel means actively paid for in parallel. Whether a phased move with a long overlap or a single switch with a short one is cheaper overall is contested, and session 3 left it open. Both end the same way: the saving begins at switch-off.

Real world

The bill for a migration nobody controlled. In December 2022 the Bank of England announced that the regulators had fined TSB "a total of £48,650,000" over its 2018 platform migration, finding that it "failed to organise and control the IT migration programme adequately." The fine came four years after the switch. The cost of a migration is not settled on cutover day: it is settled when the old system is off, the evidence is archived, and someone can show what was done and why.

Build-along

Serai's watch list and decommission log

★ 20 min · everyone builds

You need the done sheet from session 1, the parity bar from session 4 and the cutover plan from session 5. No estate of your own to hand? Do it for Serai. Dates are written as days after cutover day, C, and every timing is constructed. These two pages close the leader's half of the runbook.

List what the parity suite was weakest at. Go back to session 4's bar and ask which drift it could not see at the grain it was signed. For Serai that is the fixed offset, invisible to the monthly bar. Add anything that only arrives after cutover (the late feed) and anything the suite never read (caches, extracts, saved connections).

Give each watch item a grain, an alarm and an end. The end is a condition, not a date. Write the overall condition for closing the watch underneath.

★ Serai's watch list (constructed policy)Orders near midnight daily alarm: any of them on another day Late-feed keys nightly alarm: any key appears twice Caches and extracts each one alarm: one still serves legacy numbers First month-end close monthly alarm: a metric outside the signed bar The watch closes when: the rung-4 suite is clean on 30 consecutive nights, with no duplicated key on any of them; the first month-end close is signed inside the bar; and every cache and extract on the list is confirmed as reading the new warehouse. Owner: platform lead.

Write the five decommission steps in order, with an owner for each. Count dates from cutover day or from the step before, so the log still works if cutover moves.

★ Serai's decommission log (timings constructed)1 Legacy read-only C platform lead evidence: write grants revoked, list attached undo: minutes 2 Rollback window ends first month-end close sponsor evidence: month-end close passed rung 4, every monthly signer said same or explained, report names both targets and the signed bar undo: none 3 Final snapshot, archive step 2 + 1 day platform lead evidence: six metric totals match the last report undo: restore 4 Revoke all access step 2 + 2 days security owner evidence: who asked for access back, and why undo: minutes 5 Switch off step 4 + 60 days sponsor evidence: no request in the quiet period undo: archive only

Check the last parity report's header before anyone signs step 2. It must name what each side read. A report that compared the new warehouse with itself is all green and worthless: 0 of 5 on the bench.

Write the closing sentence and read it to whoever approved the move.

★ The closing sentenceWe stop paying for two systems on the day step 5 of the decommission log is signed. Until then the saving has not started. The rollback window closes on a written condition, the first month-end close on new passing rung 4, not on a date chosen in advance, and the last parity report names both sides.

Homework

Before you close the runbook

25 min
  1. Write your own watch list: at most five items, each with a grain, an alarm and an end condition. Mark which one your signed parity bar could not see.
  2. Find every cache, extract and saved connection that reads your warehouse today. Note which ones would keep serving old numbers after a cutover.
  3. Find the slowest reporting cycle in your business (quarterly, annual, regulatory) and decide what it means for your quiet period before switch-off.
  4. Ask the owner of the last system your organisation migrated away from whether it is actually switched off. If it is not, write down what it still costs and who could sign it off.
Check yourself

Three questions

Clear all three and Leader session 6 gets stamped in your passport.

1 · Serai's monthly numbers have matched legacy inside the signed bar since cutover. Why does the watch still read the fixed-offset drift daily?

None of the 72 shifted orders crosses a month end in this sample, so every month agrees while 74 daily numbers do not. The watch reads at the grain the parity bar could not see.

2 · When does the saving in the migration's business case start?

Until switch-off you pay for both warehouses: during the build, the parallel run and every week legacy stays ready for a rollback. Read-only legacy still costs money.

3 · The last parity report before the archive is green on every check. What must you know before the sponsor signs?

A comparison of the new warehouse with itself passes every check. Once legacy is off it can never be rerun, so the report counts only if its header names both targets, the rung and the bar.
