Ship It: Data Platform Migration · Leader session 5 of 6

Cutover day and the rollback

Cutover day is the one day a migration is judged by the people who read the numbers rather than the people who built the pipes. It goes well when nothing on it is decided for the first time. Tonight you write the three things that keep it that way for the Constructed Serai Commerce example: a go/no-go list with four gates that are either met or not, the order of the freeze from the last change on legacy to the announcement, and the rollback trigger, a number written while everyone is calm, with the name of the one person who pulls it.

Part 1 · covers the go/no-go call

Four gates, all or nothing

7 min live

A go/no-go meeting that debates is a meeting that will say go. The defence is a list of gates, each of which is met or not met on evidence someone can show, written weeks before the date so nobody is tempted to redefine "met" on the night.

LiveWhat each gate asks for, as evidence4 min▶
GateMet when you can showNot met when you hear
1 · Done sheetEvery number the business reads says "same" or "explained", with a cause and an owner. Every explained defect is fixed; only accepted differences remain, such as Serai's freight split across 90 orders."That one is still being looked at."
2 · Parity reportA rung-4 run against the signed bar, with both connection targets printed at the top. On the bench, rung 4 with the signed bar catches 5 of 5 defects with 0 false alarms."All green." With no rung, no bar and no targets on the page.
3 · RehearsalThe whole day run end to end on a copy, every step timed, and the timings written into the plan. Builder session 8 does this."Each step was tested separately."
4 · RollbackReaders moved to new and back to legacy on the copy, with the numbers checked after each move, and the trigger on paper with a name next to it."We can always switch back if we need to."

Read gate 2 carefully, because it is the one a green report fools most easily. When both sides of the comparison point at the new warehouse, which happens when a connection string is copied, the bench catches 0 of 5 defects and every check passes. Loosen the bar to 3% on every metric and even rung 4 catches only 4 of 5, missing the review scores loaded as 0. A report that does not print what each side read and which bar it used does not meet gate 2, however green it is.

LiveWhy all four, and why written in advance3 min▶

Each gate covers a different way to be wrong. The done sheet says the numbers are understood; the parity report says they were checked against a bar nobody chose on the night; the rehearsal says the day itself fits the window; the rollback test says there is a way back that has actually been walked. Three out of four is a move with one unguarded door.

In a phased move like Serai's, chosen in session 3, every wave that moves readers has its own cutover day, and each one gets the same four gates. The list is short enough to read aloud at the start of the call. Read it aloud: a gate that is met can be answered with a document, and a gate that is not met is answered with an explanation, and the room hears the difference.

Self-studyWho is on the call, and who decides3 min▶

The signers from session 2 each answer for their own lines on the done sheet: at Serai, the finance director for orders, the financial controller for item value and freight, the head of operations for items per order and the edge check, the brand lead for reviewed orders and the average review score. The data platform lead answers for gates 3 and 4. The migration lead, one named person, reads the gates and makes the call.

The rule that keeps this honest is that the decider cannot overrule a gate, only report it. If gate 2 is not met, the migration lead's job is to say "no-go, gate 2, owner, new date", not to weigh it against the cost of delay. The cost of delay was weighed when the gates were written. Serai's roles are constructed; yours will have different titles and the same shape.

Real world

Readiness that was reported, not proved. TSB's own release on the independent review of its 2018 migration notes that go-live "followed a long period of testing, with reports to the Board signalling readiness", and that the review raised "questions around how testing was carried out". Google's SRE book, describing how launches are run at scale, says plainly: "The checklist proved to be a useful tool for ensuring reproducibly reliable launches." A go/no-go list is that checklist, with one addition: every line names the evidence that meets it, so a report of readiness and proof of readiness look different on the page.

Part 2 · covers the freeze and the order of the day

Freeze, level, prove, switch, announce

6 min live

The day itself is five steps in a fixed order, and the order matters more than the hours. Each step exists so the next one compares like with like: nothing changes on legacy while you prove new matches it, and nobody reads new until the proof is in.

LiveThe five steps, and what each one protects4 min▶
  1. Freeze changes on legacy. No new SQL, no schema change, no dashboard edit on either side from the freeze until the switch. Loads keep running. A definition that changes during the final parity run turns the comparison into a comparison with a moving target.
  2. Last delta load. Bring new level with legacy: every row that arrived since the previous load, including the late-row feed. Serai's late feed is where 367 orders were once appended twice, which is why the parity run comes after this step and not before.
  3. Final parity run. The rung-4 suite against the signed bar, with both targets printed. This is the report gate 2 reads at the go/no-go call.
  4. Switch readers. Repoint what readers query from legacy to new. Builder session 8 does this as a single view swap, so the switch, and the way back, is one statement.
  5. Announce. Tell readers which numbers moved and why, from the "explained" column of the done sheet, before they find out on their own. Serai's announcement names the freight rounding policy, because the finance team will see 57 centavos and ask.

Serai's hours on the figure are constructed. The real ones come from the rehearsal: if the final parity run took two hours on the copy, the plan says two hours, and the go/no-go call is set after it, never during it.

LiveWhy legacy keeps loading after the switch2 min▶

The cheapest rollback is a repoint: readers go back to a warehouse that is still current. That only works if legacy keeps receiving the same loads as new for the whole rollback window. Stop loading it and the way back becomes a reload of every day since the switch, which is a second migration done in a hurry.

The same logic sets one more rule: during the window, nothing is written only to new. A manual adjustment, a new table an analyst builds on the first morning, a corrected figure typed into a mart: each one is a write the other side does not have, and each one makes the way back lose something. Log any such write, or hold it until the window closes.

Real world

Two copies that each took writes the other did not. In GitHub's incident of 21 October 2018, network connectivity was "restored in 43 seconds", but in that gap the database in one data centre "contained writes that were not present in the other data center". Reconciling them is why service stayed degraded for "24 hours and 11 minutes". A warehouse cutover is slower and calmer than a network partition, and the lesson transfers: once two copies have accepted different writes, going back is no longer a switch. The freeze and the no-new-only-writes rule exist to keep the way back a switch.

Part 3 · covers rollback criteria written while calm

A trigger, a name and a window

6 min live

At 23:00 on cutover day, with readers on new and the team tired, a gap in a number will look like something that can be explained by morning. That is why the rollback decision is made weeks earlier, as a rule: which numbers, how far out, who acts, and for how long the way back stays open.

LiveThree parts of a rollback rule4 min▶

The trigger is a number, tied to the done sheet. Serai's is simply the signed bar, read again after the switch: orders exact; item value and freight within 0.05%; items per order within 0.005; reviewed orders exact; average review within 0.01; and the edge check finding every one of the 595 near-midnight orders on the same day in both. Any line outside it fires the trigger. A second line covers what a check cannot see: a reader reports a number that differs and nobody can explain it before the next scheduled parity run. Both are constructed policy, built from the bar Serai already signed.

One person pulls it, and does not ask first. At Serai the migration lead repoints readers to legacy when the trigger fires, then tells the signers. The cause is found afterwards, with readers safely on numbers everyone trusts. A rule that says "the steering group will decide" is a meeting at midnight, and a meeting at midnight looks for reasons to stay.

The window ends on a written condition, not a feeling. Legacy stays loaded and read-only until the condition is met. Serai's: the first month-end close on new has passed a rung-4 parity run, and every monthly signer has said "same" or "explained". A condition, not a length: how long a parallel run or a rollback window should last is a question no source settles, and Serai's 4-week parallel run before cutover is constructed. Session 6 picks up what happens when the window closes.

LiveGradual, partial, reversible: how others roll out2 min▶

Google's SRE book describes launches this way: "Almost all updates to Google's services proceed gradually, according to a defined process", and "If the change doesn't pass the validation period, it's automatically rolled back." The SRE Workbook defines canarying as "a partial and time-limited deployment of a change in a service and its evaluation", and if the evaluation looks bad, the instruction is to "pause and roll back the deployment."

A warehouse is not a web service, and these are descriptions of one organisation's practice, not a rule for yours. The shape carries over all the same: a phased move is a partial deployment, the period after each switch is its validation, and the way back is decided before the change, not during it. Whether the whole move should be phased at all is the disagreement from session 3, and it stays a disagreement.

Self-studyTest the rollback as hard as the move3 min▶

A rollback that has never run is a hope. Gate 4 asks for it to be walked on the copy: switch readers to new, check the numbers, switch them back, check again, and time both. Builder session 8 does exactly this with a view repointed forward and back and a count after each swap.

Test the awkward cases too. Roll back after the first morning's loads have run on both sides, so you know legacy really is current. Roll back from a laptop that is not the usual one, so you know the step does not depend on one person's credentials. And confirm that readers' extracts and caches follow the pointer; a dashboard that cached new's numbers will keep showing them after you roll back. Builder session 9 returns to caches that outlive a cutover.

Real world

Choosing the numbers over the clock, in advance. GitHub's analysis of its October 2018 incident records the choice to "prioritize data integrity over site usability and time to recovery". That choice cost a long period of degraded service and protected the data. TSB's 2018 migration ended, four years later, with the Bank of England announcing fines of "a total of £48,650,000" and a finding that TSB "failed to organise and control the IT migration programme adequately." Neither case tells you what your trigger should be. Both say the trade between integrity and time is a decision someone owns, and it is better owned on paper before cutover day than in a room on the night.

Build-along

Serai's go/no-go and rollback sheet

★ 20 min · everyone builds

You need the done sheet from session 1, the signers from session 2 and the bar from session 4. No sheet to hand? Build Serai's with the numbers on this page. The result is runbook section 7 from the leader's side: one page the migration lead reads aloud at the start of the call.

Write the four gates, each with the evidence that meets it. Not "parity OK" but the document that shows it: the rung-4 report, with the bar and both targets printed at the top. Put an owner on each gate, and leave the status column empty until the evidence exists.

★ Serai's gates1 Done sheet no row "open", every defect fixed migration lead 2 Parity report rung 4, signed bar, both targets printed head of data 3 Rehearsal end to end on a copy, every step timed data platform lead 4 Rollback test readers to new and back, numbers checked data platform lead

Write the day as a sequence with an owner per step. Freeze, last delta load, final parity run, go/no-go call, switch, announce. Leave the hours blank until the rehearsal gives you real ones. Serai's on the timeline above are constructed.

Write the trigger from the bar, line by line. Copy the signed bar from session 4 and add the edge check. Add one line for a reader-reported difference nobody can explain before the next scheduled parity run.

★ Serai's rollback trigger (constructed policy)Roll back if, on any parity run after the switch: orders differ at all (monthly) item value differ by more than 0.05% freight differ by more than 0.05% items per order differ by more than 0.005 reviewed orders differ at all average review differ by more than 0.01 edge check any of the 595 near-midnight orders on another day or a reader reports a difference nobody explains before the next run.

Name the one person who pulls it, and the end condition of the window. One name, not a group. The window closes on a condition you can check, not on a date someone hopes for.

★ Who acts, and for how longPulled by: the migration lead, without asking first; signers told after. Way back: repoint the readers' view to legacy (Builder session 8). Window: legacy stays loaded and read-only until the first month-end close on new passes a rung-4 run and every monthly signer has said "same" or "explained". During it: nothing is written only to new, or every such write is logged.

Read the steering sentence to whoever approves the move.

★ The steering sentenceWe go only when all four gates are met on evidence. If any number on the done sheet leaves the signed bar after the switch, the migration lead moves readers back to legacy without a meeting, and the move waits for a new go/no-go. Legacy stays loaded until the first month-end close on new has passed.
A rollback plan that will never be used Rollback: if critical issues are identified post-cutover, the steering committee will convene to assess impact and decide whether to revert.
Homework

Before session 6

25 min
  1. Finish your go/no-go sheet: four gates, the evidence for each, an owner, and an empty status column.
  2. Write your rollback trigger from your own signed bar, one line per number on the done sheet. If a line has no tolerance yet, that is an open item for the bar, not the trigger.
  3. Find out what your readers actually query: a view, a connection string in each dashboard, a scheduled extract. Write how many places would have to change to switch them, and to switch them back.
  4. Ask who on your team would pull the rollback at midnight today. If the answer is "we would discuss it", write one name and the condition that lets them act alone.
Check yourself

Three questions

Clear all three and Leader session 5 gets stamped in your passport.

1 · At the go/no-go call, the parity report says "all checks green" but does not print what each side read. Is gate 2 met?

A comparison of new against new passes every check. Without both targets printed, a green report cannot be told apart from that break, so the gate is not met. B is the trap: the rung matters, but so does what each side read.

2 · Why does Serai keep legacy loaded, and read-only, after readers move to new?

A stale legacy turns rollback into a second migration in a hurry. B breaks the rule that nothing is written to one side only; C states a length no source gives, and Serai's 4 weeks is constructed; D replaces a written end condition with a meeting.

3 · Which rollback rule can someone act on alone at midnight?

A trigger is a number tied to the done sheet plus one named person who acts on it. A and B leave the decision to be made on the night; C has a clock but no definition of "fails", and its four hours come from nowhere.
