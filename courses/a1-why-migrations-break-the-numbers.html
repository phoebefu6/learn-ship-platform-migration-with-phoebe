Ship It: Data Platform Migration · Leader session 1 of 6

Why migrations break the numbers

A warehouse move rarely fails loudly. The jobs run, the dashboards load, and a number the board reads is quietly different. Serai Commerce, a constructed Southeast Asian commerce enabler, is moving its warehouse from a legacy stack to a new one, on the same 8,614 real orders its sibling courses use. Seven drifts are planted in the new warehouse, each one a mistake real teams make. You leave with the definition of done you will hold the move to: every number the business reads is the same, or someone can say exactly why it changed.

Six sessions, no code. Part 1 · covers where migration drift enters

Seven drifts, none of them an error

6 min live

Moving a warehouse is four steps: export what the old one holds, load it into the new one, translate the SQL that builds the tables, and point the reports at the result. Each step has its own way of changing a number while every job reports success.

LiveThe seven drifts in Serai's new warehouse4 min▶
DriftWhere it entersOrders touchedDefect or accepted
Late rows appended twice: an order delivered more than 30 days after purchase comes back on the late-row feed and is added, not mergedLoad367Defect

Case-sensitive key join: one marketplace export writes customer keys in lower case, and the new engine compares them case-sensitivelyExport and translated SQL1,767Defect
Freight split per line: order freight divided across lines and rounded per lineTranslated SQL90Accepted, cents only
Floating-point money: amounts summed as floating point, not whole centsTranslated SQLEvery orderAccepted, far below a cent

Every drift is a constructed rule, and every order it touches is real. The 72 are real orders placed between midnight and one in the morning during the two Brazilian summers in the sample; the 367 are real orders whose delivery landed more than 30 days after purchase. The two accepted differences matter as much as the five defects: a check that cannot tell them apart will raise alarms nobody acts on.

LiveWhy nothing fails loudly3 min▶

Each drift is legal SQL doing what it was told. The PostgreSQL documentation says integer division "truncates the result towards zero", while DuckDB's says that when both operands are integers, / gives a floating-point answer and // is integer division. Oracle "currently treats a character value with a length of zero as null". MySQL's default collation makes "nonbinary string comparisons" "case-insensitive by default". Move a query between engines whose defaults differ and it still compiles; it just answers a different question. Builder session 3 runs each of these in DuckDB.

That is why "the jobs are green" is not evidence. A migration is done when the numbers are, and the numbers have to be checked by something other than the job that produced them.

Real world

A bank's platform move, and the regulators' finding. In 2018 the UK bank TSB moved its customers onto a new IT platform, and the go-live went badly wrong for customers. In December 2022 the Bank of England announced that the regulators had fined TSB "a total of £48,650,000", finding that it "failed to organise and control the IT migration programme adequately." TSB's own release on the independent review it commissioned notes the reviewers raised "questions around how testing was carried out", after a go-live that "followed a long period of testing, with reports to the Board signalling readiness." Reports of readiness are not the same thing as proof.

Part 2 · covers what done means on cutover day

Same, or explained

6 min live

"The new warehouse matches the old one" is not a sentence anyone can check. "Every number on this list is the same at the grain it is read, or has a written reason and an owner" is.

LiveThree words for every number: same, explained, open4 min▶

Same means equal at the grain it is read, within a tolerance signed before anyone looked. Explained means different for a written reason someone owns, which is either fixed before cutover or accepted in writing. Open means different and nobody knows why. Done is a list with no "open" on it.

Read the figure with those words. Orders, item value and items per order are explained by defects and must be fixed, not accepted: a board pack that counts 367 orders twice is wrong, however well understood. The freight split, which moved Serai's total freight from BRL 195,579.02 to BRL 195,579.59, is explained and accepted: 57 centavos across 90 orders is a rounding policy, and the sheet says so.

Self-studyVendors say the same thing in their own words3 min▶

Migration guides from warehouse vendors converge on validating against a definition of done, though they sell their own tools for it. Google Cloud's BigQuery migration page says to "run the data validation tool to validate that your source and destination tables match", and a 2019 Google Cloud blog on migrating data warehouses asks you to "validate that the use case was successfully migrated according to your definition of done" at the end of each iteration. AWS's Database Migration Service validation "compares each row in the source with its corresponding row at the target". Read these as claims from vendors about their own products: useful for the shape of the process, never a recommendation. Session 4 and Builder sessions 4 and 5 turn "validate" into a bar you can sign.

Real world

When two copies disagree, which one is right? On 21 October 2018 a network fault split GitHub's data centres for 43 seconds, and when the link came back, each side's database "contained writes that were not present in the other data center". GitHub's post-incident analysis records that the team chose to "prioritize data integrity over site usability and time to recovery", and service was degraded for "24 hours and 11 minutes". That is the decision a migration lead pre-commits to: when the two systems disagree, the numbers win over the schedule.

Part 3 · covers what each rung of checks catches

Five rungs, one blind spot

5 min live

The bench plants each drift alone and asks a growing suite of checks whether it noticed. A defect that raises an alarm is caught. An accepted difference that raises one is a false alarm. The ladder is not a straight climb.

LiveReading the ladder as a leader3 min▶

Rung 0, row counts: only the late rows change the count, from 8,614 to 8,981. Rung 1, keys: the same number of rows, but 1,767 orders whose customer key no longer resolves. Rung 2, every metric every day, exact: catches all five defects and also flags the two accepted differences: the freight split on 80 days and floating-point item value on 302 days. Rung 3, Serai's signed bar at the monthly grain the board reads: the false alarms go quiet, and so does the time-zone drift, because in this sample none of the 72 shifted orders crosses a month end. Rung 4, an edge check on the 595 orders placed within an hour of midnight: 72 land on another day, so the drift is caught again.

The lesson for the person signing: ask which rung the "all green" report came from. Sessions 4 and 6 return to the two awkward rungs.

LiveTwo ways to make the bench lie2 min▶

Loosen the bar to 3% on every metric and the suite misses the missing-review drift as well: 3 of 5 at rung 3 and 4 of 5 even at rung 4, because no month moves by more than 2.22%. Point both sides of the comparison at the new warehouse, a mistake that happens when a connection string is copied, and every check passes: 0 of 5. A green parity report means nothing until you know what each side read and how wide the bar was.

Real world

The fault that hides in a monthly number. The 72 shifted orders exist because Brazil observed daylight saving in the summers of 2016-17 and 2017-18, and a fixed -03:00 offset is right for the rest of the year. The IANA time zone database's 2019b release records that "Brazil no longer observes DST", so code written since then can hard-code -03:00 and pass every recent test while still misplacing historical orders. Builder session 9 is about drift like this.

Build-along

Your done sheet

★ 20 min · everyone builds

You need the list of reports your business reads every month, and ten minutes with whoever owns the warehouse. No list to hand? Do it for Serai with the numbers on this page. The sheet becomes page one of the migration runbook.

List the numbers people read, not the tables you own. Start from the board pack, the finance close and the three dashboards with the most viewers. One line per number. Serai's list starts with orders, item value, items per order, reviewed orders and average review score.

Write the grain each one is read at. Daily, weekly, monthly. This is where the time-zone drift lives: Serai's daily numbers move on 74 days, its monthly numbers on none.

★ Serai's sheet, first two columnsOrders monthly board pack Item value monthly finance close Items per order daily ops dashboard Reviewed orders monthly brand reviews Average review monthly brand reviews

Name who reads it. The reader is the person who will notice on day one, and the person who signs that row off.

Leave the last column empty until there is evidence. It fills in from the parity checks in later sessions. For practice, fill one row from this page: average review score, explained by NULL loaded as 0, to be fixed before cutover, owned by the data platform lead.

Write the steering sentence and read it to whoever approves the move.

★ The steering sentenceWe cut over when every number on the done sheet says "same" at the grain it is read, or "explained" with a written cause and an owner, and every explained defect is fixed. A green job run is not evidence. A parity report counts only when it names the rung, the bar and what each side read.
The definition of done that proves nothing Migration complete when all pipelines run successfully in the new environment and stakeholders confirm the dashboards look right.
Homework

Before session 2

25 min

  2. For one number on it, find out whether any query behind it divides two whole numbers, compares text keys, or converts a time zone. Write which.

Check yourself

Three questions

Clear all three and Leader session 1 gets stamped in your passport.

1 · On the bench, a row-count check catches only 1 of the 5 planted defects. Why?

Counts move only when rows are added or dropped: 8,614 against 8,981 for the late rows. Integer division, NULL as 0, the time-zone shift and the case-sensitive join leave the count exactly equal.

2 · Serai's monthly numbers all match legacy within the signed bar. What does that prove about its daily numbers?

The 72 shifted orders move to the previous day without crossing a month end, so every month agrees while 74 daily numbers do not. C sounds reasonable, but Serai's ops dashboard reads daily, so the drift is not harmless.

3 · Freight moved from BRL 195,579.02 to BRL 195,579.59 across 90 orders because of per-line rounding. What goes in the done sheet?

A known cause with a size and an owner is "explained". Calling it "same" hides a policy decision; calling it open or a defect turns an accepted difference into a false alarm.
